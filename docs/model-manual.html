Part I — Overall information

What P-TRANSMIT AI is

P-TRANSMIT AI is an open, locally-hostable decision-support platform for Plasmodium falciparum transmission, genomic epidemiology and antimalarial drug resistance surveillance in Ghana. It ingests weekly surveillance data (cases plus environmental covariates), trains and compares supervised forecasting models, produces per-region forecasts with uncertainty ranges, explains what drove each prediction, and raises relative early-warning signals for human review.

Scientific governance principles (enforced in code)

  • Synthetic data are always labelled and never mixed with real data.
  • No invented thresholds. Risk categories are relative to the loaded dataset's own history; resistance interpretation requires researcher-supplied definitions.
  • Strictly temporal validation. Time-series rows are never shuffled; train weeks always precede test weeks.
  • MAE / RMSE are primary metrics for case counts; R² is reported for completeness only.
  • Explanations describe model behaviour, not causal effects.
  • Alerts are research signals, not confirmed outbreaks; they require human and public-health review.
  • Genetic marker ≠ phenotypic drug resistance.
  • Audit logs record logins, dataset operations, training runs and report generation.

Part II — The AI model: what each layer means

The "AI model" is not a single neural network — it is a pipeline of ten layers. Each layer has one job; together they turn raw weekly surveillance rows into forecasts, explanations and early-warning signals.

1 · Data — standardised weekly rows
2 · Features — lags, environment, season, region
3 · Models — RF · XGBoost · Ridge
4 · Validation — chronological split
5 · Metrics — MAE / RMSE primary
6 · Forecast — next weeks per region
7 · Uncertainty — residual-based intervals
8 · Risk — relative categories
9 · Explainability — SHAP / perturbation
10 · Alerts — relative early-warning rules

Layer 1Data layer — the raw material

What it is: a weekly surveillance table, one row per region per epi-week.

  • Required columns: region, week_start_date, malaria_cases (synonyms auto-detected, e.g. mal_cases, cases, date).
  • Optional covariates: rainfall_mm, temperature_mean_c, humidity_pct, ndvi, population_illustrative, suspected_cases, test_positivity_rate.
  • Ingestion profiles data quality, suggests column mappings and fuzzy-matches names to Ghana's 16 canonical regions. Nothing is imputed silently — missing values are reported.
What it means: garbage in → garbage out. The Data Explorer's quality report is your first checkpoint before training.

Layer 2Feature layer — what the model actually sees

For every region-week, the engine (build_supervised) constructs:

Feature groupColumnsMeaning
Case history (lags)cases_lag1…cases_lag4Confirmed cases in each of the previous 1–4 weeks (per region). The dominant predictor: recent momentum.
Environment (current + lag)rainfall_mm, temperature_mean_c, humidity_pct, ndvi (+ _lag1 of each)Weather/vegetation conditions now and one week ago — known correlates of transmission.
Seasonalitysin_week, cos_weekCyclic encoding of the ISO week-of-year (period 52) so the model can learn wet/dry-season patterns without a discontinuity at year boundaries.
Region fixed effectsregion_… dummies (one per region)One-hot indicator for the row's region — lets a single global model capture each region's baseline level.
Population (optional)population_illustrativeDenominator/weight column when present.
Targettarget_lead = cases at week t+hFor horizon h, the label is the case count h weeks in the future (a lead shift, shift(-h)).
What it means: the model predicts the future from (a) recent case history, (b) current environment, (c) time of year, and (d) which region it is. Rows with missing lag/lead values are dropped — that is why the first weeks of each region are not used.

Layer 3Model layer — three competing algorithms

Each horizon trains three candidate models (DEFAULT_MODELS); the platform keeps the best one:

ModelHyper-parameters (fixed in v0.1)Plain-language explanation
Ridge StandardScaler → Ridge(α = 1.0) A regularised linear model. Learns one weight per feature: stable, fast, fully interpretable, but only captures linear relationships. Serves as the honest baseline.
Random Forest 250 trees, min_samples_leaf = 2, seed fixed Averaged ensemble of decision trees ("bagging"). Captures non-linearity and interactions (e.g. rainfall × season × region), robust to outliers, rarely overfits hard. Slower and less interpretable than Ridge.
XGBoost 500 trees, learning-rate 0.05, depth 4, subsample 0.9, colsample 0.9, λ 1.0 Gradient boosting: trees added sequentially, each correcting the previous one's errors. Usually the strongest on tabular data; the most sensitive to settings and to data quality.
  • Training happens once per horizon (1, 2, 4, 8 weeks) — so up to 4 × 3 = 12 fits per run.
  • The winner per horizon = lowest test-set MAE. It is persisted as a joblib artifact, e.g. transmission_xgboost_h4_ds1.joblib, together with its full provenance (feature list, train/test weeks, seed, software versions, timestamp).
  • A fixed random seed (default 42, user-settable) makes runs reproducible.
What it means: you don't pick the algorithm — you offer candidates and the platform picks the most accurate on held-out future weeks, per horizon, and records exactly how.

Layer 4Validation layer — honest evaluation

  • Chronological split on unique weeks (temporal_split): the earliest 80% of weeks (configurable "train fraction") form the training set; the latest 20% form the test set.
  • Train-week and test-week sets are disjoint and max(train week) < min(test week) — verified by the automated test suite. No future information can leak backwards.
  • Lags are computed within each region's own history before the split, so no row borrows information from a test week.
What it means: reported metrics come from genuinely unseen future weeks — the score approximates how the model would have behaved in production, not how well it memorised the past.

Layer 5Metrics layer — how good is good?

MetricFormula / ruleHow to read it
MAE (primary)mean |predicted − observed|"Typical error in cases per week." Compare it to your region's case scale: MAE 8 on counts of ~100 ≈ 8% typical miss.
RMSE (primary)√mean(error²)Like MAE but punishes large misses heavily — a big gap between RMSE and MAE signals occasional bad predictions.
MAPEmean |err/observed| on non-zero weeksPercentage error; unstable when counts are near zero, hence excluded on zero weeks.
sMAPEsymmetric % variantBounded percentage variant for comparison across regions.
R²coefficient of determinationReported for completeness only; low R² is normal and not alarming for noisy count data.
residual_stdstd of test residualsFeeds the uncertainty intervals (Layer 7).
n_test, fit_seconds—How many held-out rows were scored; how long fitting took.
What it means: judge models by MAE/RMSE in case units, per horizon — longer horizons (8 weeks) are inherently harder; expect their errors to be larger.

Layer 6Forecast layer — producing next weeks' numbers

  • At the last observed week, the engine builds one feature row per region (lags from actual history, seasonality from the calendar, region dummy = 1).
  • Each persisted best model predicts the case count h weeks ahead (h ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}).
  • Environmental persistence assumption: future weather/vegetation are held at their last observed value. The API states this in every forecast response (assumptions field) — the model does not know next month's rainfall.
  • Predictions are clipped at ≥ 0 (case counts cannot be negative).
What it means: a forecast is "the best guess given conditions stay roughly as they are" — read it alongside the stated assumptions, not as a certainty.

Layer 7Uncertainty layer — the error bars

  • Each forecast carries interval_low / interval_high = prediction ± 1.96 × residual_std (low clipped at 0).
  • residual_std is the standard deviation of the model's errors on the held-out test weeks — i.e. "how wrong this model has actually been on unseen data."
  • Method string returned with every forecast: "residual-based Gaussian approximation (not calibrated probabilistic intervals)."
What it means: the interval shows the model's typical error range — it is not a 95% probability guarantee, and it will be wide where the model is shaky. Treat values near the upper bound as "worth watching."

Layer 8Risk layer — relative categories

ContextRule (computed from the loaded dataset itself)
Forecast rowspredicted ≥ historical p90 → very high · ≥ p75 → high · ≥ p50 → moderate · else low (quantiles of all weekly cases in the dataset).
Map "risk" layerPer region, relative to that region's own historical p90: ≥ p90 → very high · ≥ 0.9·p90 → high · ≥ 0.75·p90 → moderate · else low.
What it means: categories answer "unusual for this data?" — they are not national clinical thresholds. A "very high" in a chronically low region may still be a small number; always read it with the case values next to it.

Layer 9Explainability layer — "why this number?"

  • Global importance: permutation importance on the held-out weeks (5 repeats, scored by MAE change) — which features the model relies on overall; shown as top-12.
  • Per-region explanations (Dashboard "Why?" panel): the engine perturbs each non-region feature (replaces it with its training median) and records how the prediction moves; where shap is installed, tree models use SHAP TreeExplainer instead. Top 8 contributions per region are shown as signed bars.
  • Positive contribution = pushed the prediction up; negative = pulled it down.
  • Method text always ends with: "model behaviour, not causal effects."
What it means: explanations tell you what the model leaned on — useful for sanity-checking ("is it reacting to rainfall or to last week's spike?") — but they are not evidence that feature caused transmission changes.

Layer 10Alert layer — relative early-warning rules

Three deterministic rules scan every region (minimum 30 weeks of history) — see alerts.py:

RuleSeverityTrigger
LevelwarningMean of the last 4 observed weeks > that region's historical 90th percentile.
TrendwarningLeast-squares slope of the last 8 weeks > the 90th percentile of all historical 8-week slopes for that region (and threshold > 0).
ForecastinfoMedian model prediction for weeks 1–4 > the region's historical p90.
  • Every alert stores its evidence (the numbers that fired it), the model reference and a plain-language explanation.
  • Duplicates are suppressed: an open alert of the same type+region is not re-created on the next scan.
  • Workflow: open → acknowledged / dismissed — a person always decides.
What it means: alerts are "unusual relative to this dataset" flags for triage — never confirmation of an outbreak, and never a treatment directive.

Part III — Operating the model (step by step)

A. Access the platform

1

Local: double-click start-ptransmit.bat → open http://localhost:4173 (both server windows must stay open; close them to stop).

Hosted: open your permanent deployment URL (e.g. the Render link) — the same UI and API are served from one address.

2

Sign in: click ⚡ Enter live demo (read-only researcher, no account), or use demo / demo1234 or admin / ptransmit-admin. Change real credentials before any real deployment.

B. Train the models (Model Laboratory)

1

Open Data Explorer and confirm your dataset is listed (the clearly-labelled DEMO dataset loads automatically in demo mode).

2

Open Model Laboratory and set:

  • Dataset — the surveillance table to learn from.
  • Random seed (default 42) — same seed ⇒ same results.
  • Train fraction (temporal) (default 0.8) — earliest 80% of weeks train, latest 20% test.
  • Models — tick Random Forest, XGBoost, Ridge (all three by default).
  • Horizons (weeks) — 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 (all by default).
3

Press Train and wait (~1 minute on a laptop for the demo dataset; longer on free-host CPUs). Training continues per horizon even if one model errors.

4

Read the results table: for each horizon you get every candidate's MAE/RMSE/R², the best model (lowest test MAE) and its saved artifact path. Expand a row's Provenance for dataset, features, seed, versions and timestamp.

Training has run once already? The Transmission AI page has the same train button plus the region/model selectors for inspecting held-out curves — useful for a second opinion before trusting a horizon.

C. View forecasts

1

Dashboard: KPI cards (last week, recent 4-week national cases, national forecast next week, regions at elevated risk) and the map. Use the Map layer selector: Transmission risk category / Recent 4-week case mean / Forecast median (≤4 weeks). Hover any region for detail.

2

Spatial Intelligence: the same map with a snapshot table of all regions (observed, historical p90, forecast, risk).

3

Transmission AI: per region and per model, held-out observed-vs-predicted curves and the metric block — check these before quoting any forecast.

D. Read the explanations

1

Dashboard → "Why? Top contributors for ⟨region⟩": signed bars of the top features behind that region's current prediction, with the method note above them. Ask: is the model leaning on recent cases, rainfall, or season?

E. Run and triage alerts

1

Open Alerts → Run alert scan. New signals appear with severity, region, evidence numbers and the explanation text.

2

Review each signal against your operational knowledge, then Acknowledge (keeping it on the radar) or Dismiss (not actionable). Already-open signals of the same type+region are not duplicated on re-scan.

F. Load real data & retrain

1

Data Explorer → choose record type surveillance → upload CSV/XLSX/JSON with region, week_start_date, malaria_cases (synonyms auto-detected). Review the data-quality report and column mapping; confirm region matches.

2

Model Laboratory → select the new dataset → Train. Dashboard, forecasts, explanations and alerts now default to the latest dataset.

3

Regenerate a Report for the record (Markdown, includes limitations), and check Settings for demo-mode status and the governance checklist.

Housekeeping on free/hosted tiers: storage is ephemeral — after a restart the labelled demo data re-seeds automatically, but trained model files and uploaded accounts do not survive. Re-train via Model Laboratory after every redeploy. Locally, artifacts live in models/ and persist across restarts.

Part IV — Interpreting outputs & limitations

You seeRead it asDo NOT read it as

Known limitations (v0.1)

  • Environmental persistence: forecasts hold weather/vegetation at the last observed value; genuine rainfall shocks will not be anticipated.
  • Direct (non-recursive) horizons: each horizon is a separate model; an 8-week forecast does not internally compound the 1–4 week ones.
  • Intervals are uncalibrated Gaussian approximations from test residuals.
  • Regional dummy coverage: a region absent at training time gets no dedicated dummy — add history for all 16 regions before real use.
  • Minimum history: alert rules need ≥ 30 weeks; lag construction consumes the first 4+ weeks per region.
  • Demo data are synthetic (with a scripted late-season upswing to exercise alerts) and always labelled.
  • Genomics/Resistance modules are descriptive scaffolds awaiting validated data; a detected marker is not phenotypic resistance.
Bottom line: P-TRANSMIT AI supports research and surveillance judgement. Predictions are not diagnoses and must not independently determine treatment or public-health action.

Appendix

A. Anatomy of a trained artifact

File: models/transmission_<type>_h<horizon>_ds<dataset_id>.joblib containing:

FieldContents
modelThe fitted scikit-learn / XGBoost estimator.
model_type, horizonWhich algorithm and lead time it serves.
feature_namesExact column order the model expects (lags, environment, season, region dummies).
metricsMAE, RMSE, MAPE, sMAPE, R², residual_std, n_test, fit_seconds.
feature_importancePermutation-importance ranking on held-out weeks.
train_weeks, test_weeksThe precise chronological split used — reproducibility proof.
seed, software, trained_atRandom seed, Python/scikit-learn/XGBoost/pandas/numpy versions, UTC timestamp.

B. Forecast row fields

region, horizon_weeks, last_observed_week, forecast_week, predicted_cases (clipped ≥ 0), interval_low/interval_high (±1.96·residual_std), risk_category, model_type, interval_method — plus the response-level assumptions string (environmental persistence).

C. Glossary

TermMeaning
Horizon (h)How many weeks ahead are being predicted (1, 2, 4, 8).
LagA past value of the same series (e.g. cases one week ago).
Lead / target_leadThe future value being predicted (shift −h).
Temporal splitTrain on earlier weeks, test on later weeks — no shuffle, no leakage.
Quantile (p50/p75/p90)The value below which 50/75/90% of historical observations fall — the basis of relative risk bands.
Permutation importanceHow much held-out accuracy drops when a feature's values are shuffled — a model-agnostic "who matters" ranking.
SHAPGame-theory-based feature attribution for tree models; used when the optional shap package is installed.
ResidualPrediction − observed on held-out weeks; residual_std sizes the intervals.
ProvenanceThe recorded context of a training run (data, split, seed, versions) that makes it reproducible.
